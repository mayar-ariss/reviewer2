# Headings: ICMJE on the sections of a research article

Load this file when the user asks what the main sections of a research article should be called, whether to use subheadings, or which section a piece of information belongs in. It applies most directly to medical journals.

**Every rule below is the ICMJE's.** Rules cite the Recommendations by section. Where the ICMJE gives no rule, this file says so rather than supplying one. Nothing here is a general writing preference.

## Sources and how they are cited

| Short form | Work |
|---|---|
| ICMJE | International Committee of Medical Journal Editors. *Recommendations for the Conduct, Reporting, Editing, and Publication of Scholarly Work in Medical Journals*. Section IV.A, "Preparing a Manuscript for Submission to a Medical Journal": https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/manuscript-preparation/preparing-for-submission.html |

The Recommendations are written for medical journals and the authors who submit to them. Outside medicine, treat them as one well-defined standard and check the target journal's own instructions first.

## How to apply this file

*Procedure, not a rule of the ICMJE's.*

1. **Ask for the target journal and its article type**, and read its instructions for authors. The journal's instructions take precedence over this file.
2. **List the draft's headings and subheadings**, and check them against rules 1–3.
3. **Check what sits in Methods and what in Results** (rule 4), and the ethics statement (rule 5).
4. **Propose revisions citing the rule.** Do not rename sections against the journal's instructions.

## Rules

### 1. Use the IMRAD sections for original research

*Source: ICMJE, IV.A.1.*

The text of original research articles is usually divided into Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion. The ICMJE calls this structure "a reflection of the process of scientific discovery," not an arbitrary format (IV.A.1).

- Check that an original research article has these four main sections, under these names or the journal's equivalents.

### 2. Use another format where the article type calls for it

*Source: ICMJE, IV.A.1.*

Other types of article may need different formats: meta-analyses may require a different structure, and case reports, narrative reviews and editorials may be less structured or unstructured (IV.A.1).

- Before imposing IMRAD, check the article type.

### 3. Use subheadings within sections where they organize the content

*Source: ICMJE, IV.A.1 and IV.A.3.d.*

Articles often need subheadings within the IMRAD sections to organize their content (IV.A.1). The ICMJE arranges its own guidance on Methods under three headings: Selection and Description of Participants; Data Collection and Measurements; and Statistics (IV.A.3.d.i–iii).

- Where the journal follows the ICMJE and names no Methods subheadings of its own, check that the Methods section covers each of the three areas. Using them as subheadings is this skill's suggestion, not an ICMJE requirement.
- Whether a Methods section needs subheadings at all depends on how extended it is: see `rules/methods.md`, rule 4. How to group and order subsections: see `rules/methods-structure.md`.

### 4. Keep information obtained during the study in Results

*Source: ICMJE, IV.A.3.d.*

The guiding principle of the Methods section is clarity about how and why a study was done in a particular way. In general, it should include only information that was available when the study plan or protocol was written; all information obtained during the study belongs in Results (IV.A.3.d).

- Flag findings, measurements or observations from the study reported in Methods, and ask whether they belong in Results.
- `rules/methods-structure.md` (rule 5) allows Methods to report incidental observations the results do not rest on. Where a journal follows the ICMJE, its principle is the stricter one.

### 5. State ethics approval in Methods

*Source: ICMJE, IV.A.3.d.*

The Methods section should state that the research was approved by an independent review body, such as an ethics committee or institutional review board (IV.A.3.d).

- For research with human or animal participants, check that the approval is stated in Methods, and ask the author for it if it is missing.

## Where the ICMJE gives no rule

The Recommendations name the four main sections and the areas Methods should cover, but give no rule on the wording, numbering or depth of subheadings. They do not cover the conventions of non-medical fields. What each Methods area must contain in detail is covered by `rules/reporting.md` and the guideline for the study design, not here. This file adds nothing where the ICMJE is silent.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] The journal's instructions for authors were checked first
- [ ] An original research article has the four IMRAD sections, or the article type calls for another format (rules 1–2)
- [ ] Subheadings organize the content; ICMJE's three Methods areas are covered where the journal follows the ICMJE (rule 3)
- [ ] Information obtained during the study is in Results (rule 4)
- [ ] Ethics approval is stated in Methods where required (rule 5)
