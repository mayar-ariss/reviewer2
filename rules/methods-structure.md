# Methods structure: Cotos, Huffman and Link's DRaC model

Load this file when the user wants to check what a Methods section contains and in what order: which parts are missing, how subsections are grouped and ordered, and where rationales and side observations sit. Load it together with `rules/methods.md`.

**Every rule below is Cotos, Huffman and Link's.** Rules cite the authors' accepted manuscript by section, because the manuscript's page numbers differ from the journal's. Where they give no rule, this file says so rather than supplying one. Nothing here is a general writing preference.

## Sources and how they are cited

| Short form | Work |
|---|---|
| CHL 2017 | Cotos, E., Huffman, S., & Link, S. (2017). A move/step model for methods sections: Demonstrating rigour and credibility. *English for Specific Purposes*, 46, 90–106. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esp.2017.01.001 Cited from the accepted manuscript, Iowa State University Digital Repository (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0): https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/entities/publication/c038e897-339f-4815-8631-f5e6b8db8443 |

The model was built from the Methods sections of 900 research articles, 30 from each of 30 disciplines, in the tradition of Swales's genre analysis; its name echoes Swales's CARS model for introductions (CHL 2017, abstract and §4.1). Disciplinary experts reviewed the model, and three annotators coded the corpus with high agreement (§3.2).

## How to apply this file

*Procedure, not a rule of the authors'.*

1. **Ask for the field and the target journal**, and read the Methods sections of a few recent articles in it: disciplines differ in which steps they use and how they sequence the moves (§4.2.3–4.2.4).
2. **Label the draft.** Mark each subsection and paragraph with its move and step (see the model). Show the user the labelled outline.
3. **Check the labelled outline against rules 1–5.**
4. **Propose revisions citing the rule.** Do not invent settings, subjects, data, tools, variables or rationales; where a step is needed, ask the author.
5. **Run the checklist** at the end.

## The model

Demonstrating Rigour and Credibility (DRaC): three moves and sixteen steps (CHL 2017, §4.1). The numbering of moves and steps does not indicate their order (§4.1).

| Move | Steps | Function (§4.1.1–4.1.3) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Contextualizing study methods** | 1 Referencing previous works · 2 Providing general information · 3 Identifying the methodological approach · 4 Describing the setting · 5 Introducing the subjects/participants · 6 Rationalizing pre-experiment decisions | Gives the background for the research approach and frames the description of the study and the analysis of the data |
| **2. Describing the study** | 1 Acquiring the data · 2 Describing the data · 3 Describing experimental/study procedures · 4 Describing tools · 5 Identifying variables · 6 Rationalizing experiment decisions · 7 Reporting incrementals | Details what was done before data analysis: conditions, treatments, controls, data, tools and decisions taken during the study |
| **3. Establishing credibility** | 1 Preparing the data · 2 Describing the data analysis · 3 Rationalizing data processing/analysis | Explains how the data were analysed and why the analysis can be trusted |

In the corpus, Move 2 made up 71% of annotated units, Move 1 22% and Move 3 7% (§4.2.1).

## Rules

### 1. Include the study description; scale the context and the credibility moves to the field

*Source: CHL 2017, §4.2.1.*

Move 2 is obligatory. Every text in the corpus contained Move 1, so the choice for Move 1 is how much of it to give, not whether to include it. Some writers in some disciplines left out Move 3, which their communities tolerate when Moves 1 and 2 cover the study adequately (§4.2.1).

- Flag a Methods section with no description of the study (Move 2), or no context for it at all (Move 1).
- Where Move 3 is absent, ask whether the target journal's Methods sections include it.

### 2. Cover the steps the study's nature requires

*Source: CHL 2017, §4.2.2 and §5.*

The authors stress that it is the nature of the research, not rhetorical convention, that determines how a methodology should be reported (§5). Describing procedures and describing tools are the most frequent steps in Move 2, but writers in all disciplines also give brief details of data sources, data characteristics and variables (§4.2.2).

- For each step in the model, ask whether the study involved it (a setting, subjects, acquired data, tools, variables, data preparation, analysis) and whether the draft reports it. List the steps the study needs but the draft lacks, and ask the author for them.

### 3. Order the moves so context frames the description, and analysis follows it

*Source: CHL 2017, §4.1 and §4.2.3.*

Methods sections are not linearly structured (§4.2.3). The numbering of the moves does not fix their order, although that order may be a logical way to structure the content (§4.1). Writers commonly cycle between Moves 1 and 2, and Move 3, where present, tends to follow Move 2 (§4.2.3). Disciplines differ: in immunobiology most sections open with brief context and close with short credibility statements, while in the geological and atmospheric sciences Move 3 also appears throughout the section (§4.2.3).

- Use the labelled outline to check the order of subsections. Flag analysis (Move 3) placed before the description of the data and procedures it analyses, and ask whether the target journal does this.
- Do not flag a return to context (Move 1) within the description: cycling between Moves 1 and 2 is common.

### 4. Put each rationale with the decision it explains

*Source: CHL 2017, §4.1.1–4.1.3.*

The model has a rationalizing step in each move: for decisions taken before the study (Move 1, step 6), for decisions taken while it was carried out (Move 2, step 6), and for choices of data processing and analysis (Move 3, step 3) (§4.1.1–4.1.3).

- Check that each rationale sits with the decision it explains: a sampling rationale with the subjects, an analysis rationale with the analysis.
- How many rationales the section needs is a question of how extended it is: see `rules/methods.md`, rule 6.

### 5. Keep incrementals to observations the results do not rest on

*Source: CHL 2017, §4.1.2.*

Reporting incrementals (Move 2, step 7) covers observations made during the procedure that are not critical to the study's results and are therefore not reported in the Results section, such as incremental measurements or preliminary qualitative observations (§4.1.2).

- For each observation or measurement reported in Methods, ask whether the paper's findings rest on it. If they do, it belongs in Results.

## Where Cotos, Huffman and Link give no rule

The authors give no rule on the wording of headings or subheadings; they note only that subheadings in Methods sections vary, citing unconventional subheadings found in applied linguistics (§2). They did not analyse the language that realizes each move and step (§5). Their corpus excluded mathematics, statistics, architecture and history, whose conventions differ from experimental reports (note 3); mathematics papers may have no Methods section at all (§2). The manuscript numbers the rationalizing step of Move 2 inconsistently (step 6 in §4.1.2, step 7 in §4.2.5); this file follows the definitions in §4.1. This file adds nothing where the authors are silent.

## Example

*An illustration of this file's procedure, not the authors'.* Methods outline of a hydraulic modelling paper: "2.1 Model performance; 2.2 Study area; 2.3 Model set-up; 2.4 Boundary conditions."

- **Labels:** 2.1 Move 3 (credibility) or results; 2.2 Move 1, step 4 (setting); 2.3 Move 2, steps 3–4 (procedures, tools); 2.4 Move 2, steps 1–2 (data).
- **Order (rule 3):** the analysis comes before the study it analyses. Ask whether 2.1 should follow 2.4, or whether it reports findings that belong in Results (rule 5).
- **Missing steps (rule 2):** no data preparation or analysis step for calibration. Ask the author how the model was calibrated and evaluated.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] The draft was labelled by move and step, and the outline shown to the user
- [ ] Move 2 is present; Moves 1 and 3 are scaled to the journal (rule 1)
- [ ] Every step the study involved is reported, or the author was asked (rule 2)
- [ ] Analysis follows the description it depends on, unless the journal does otherwise (rule 3)
- [ ] Each rationale sits with its decision (rule 4)
- [ ] Observations the findings rest on are in Results, not Methods (rule 5)
- [ ] No setting, data, tool, variable or rationale was invented
