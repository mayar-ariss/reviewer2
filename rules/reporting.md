# Reporting: EQUATOR reporting guidelines

Load this file when the user wants to know whether a draft reports what readers, reviewers and later systematic reviewers need: whether the Methods and Results contain the minimum information for the study's design. **Scope: health research only.** EQUATOR's reporting guidelines are written for health research; do not apply this file to papers in other fields.

**Every rule below is the EQUATOR Network's.** Rules cite EQUATOR's own pages. The checklists themselves are long and change between versions, so this file points to them rather than reproducing them. Where EQUATOR gives no rule, this file says so rather than supplying one. Nothing here is a general writing preference.

*Sources and how they are cited are listed at the end of this file.*

## How to apply this file

*Procedure, not an EQUATOR rule.*

1. **Identify the study design** from the draft, and ask the author if it is unclear (rule 2).
2. **Find the matching guideline** in EQUATOR's library, in its current version, and open its checklist (rule 2).
3. **Go through the checklist item by item** against the draft, noting for each whether it is reported, and where (rule 3).
4. **List the missing items** for the author. Do not supply the missing information; ask for it.
5. **Run the checklist** at the end of this file.

## Rules

### 1. Treat a reporting guideline as the minimum, not the ideal

*Source: EQUATOR definition.*

EQUATOR defines a reporting guideline as a checklist, flow diagram or structured text that guides authors in reporting a specific type of research, developed using explicit methodology. A guideline gives the minimum information a manuscript needs so that it can be understood by readers, replicated by researchers, used by clinicians to make decisions, and included in systematic reviews.

- Use the guideline to find what is missing, not as a ceiling on what may be reported.

### 2. Match the guideline to the study design

*Source: EQUATOR home.*

EQUATOR's key reporting guidelines, by study design:

| Study design | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Randomised trials; their protocols | CONSORT; SPIRIT |
| Observational studies (cohort, case-control, cross-sectional) | STROBE |
| Systematic reviews and meta-analyses; their protocols; meta-analyses of observational studies | PRISMA; PRISMA-P; MOOSE |
| Diagnostic accuracy studies; prediction models | STARD; TRIPOD (including TRIPOD+AI) |
| Qualitative research | SRQR; COREQ |
| Case reports | CARE |
| Animal research | ARRIVE |
| Quality improvement in healthcare | SQUIRE |
| Economic evaluations | CHEERS |

- Name the guideline that fits the draft's design, and check EQUATOR's library for an extension that fits better (for example, for a particular type of trial).

### 3. Check every item, and report where each is addressed

*Source: EQUATOR definition.*

A reporting guideline presents a clear list of the items a paper should include (EQUATOR definition).

- For each checklist item, record whether the draft reports it and where (section, paragraph).
- Where an item does not apply to the study, note why, so that its absence is not mistaken for an omission (this skill's practice, not an EQUATOR rule).

## Where EQUATOR gives no rule

Reporting guidelines say what to report; the EQUATOR pages used here say nothing about how results should be interpreted or how conclusions should be worded (for that, see `rules/spin.md` and `rules/claims.md`), or about how a section should be written (see the section files). EQUATOR covers health research; for designs and fields without a guideline, this file has nothing to check. This file adds nothing where EQUATOR is silent.

## Example

*An illustration of this file's procedure, not EQUATOR's.* A draft reports "a randomized trial of 120 patients" with no account of how participants were allocated, whether anyone was blinded, or how many were lost to follow-up.

- **Design and guideline (rule 2):** a randomised trial, so CONSORT, in its current version.
- **Items (rule 3):** the checklist asks for the randomisation and allocation procedures, blinding, and the flow of participants through the trial; the draft reports none of them. List them for the author, and ask for the information.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] The guideline was used as the minimum to report, not as a ceiling (rule 1)
- [ ] The study design was identified, with the author if needed (rule 2)
- [ ] The matching guideline, and any better-fitting extension, was named (rule 2)
- [ ] Each checklist item was checked against the draft, with its location recorded (rule 3)
- [ ] Items that do not apply were noted with a reason (rule 3)
- [ ] Missing items were listed for the author; no information was supplied
